## Pocono Springs Civic Association v. MacKenzie (Superior Court of Pennsylvania, 1995)

**Facts**
- On October 14, 1969, the MacKenzies purchased a vacant lot in Pocono Springs Development.
- In 1987, the MacKenzies attempted to sell their lot.
- A potential buyer conditioned the purchase on the lot being suitable for an on-lot sewage system.
- An inspection revealed the lot had inadequate soil for proper percolation, and the sale was lost.
- The MacKenzies attempted to give the lot to the Pocono Springs Civic Association, but the Association declined.
- The MacKenzies then tried to persuade the Association to accept the lot as a gift for a park, but the Association again declined.
- In 1986, the MacKenzies stopped paying real estate taxes on the lot.
- In 1988, the Wayne County Tax Claim Bureau offered the property for sale due to delinquent taxes, but there were no purchasers.
- In 1990, the lot was again offered for sale by the Tax Claim Bureau, but it was not sold and was placed on its "repository" list.
- The MacKenzies signed a notarized statement expressing their desire to abandon the lot and mailed it to "all interested parties."
- The MacKenzies do not accept mail regarding the property and have not visited the lot or used the development's services since 1986.
- The Pocono Springs Civic Association sued the MacKenzies for unpaid association fees.

**Procedural History**
- The trial court granted summary judgment in favor of the Pocono Springs Civic Association.
- The MacKenzies appealed to the Superior Court of Pennsylvania.

**Issue**
- Can real property owned in fee simple with perfect title be abandoned under Pennsylvania law?

**Holding**
- The Pocono Springs Civic Association wins. Real property owned in fee simple with perfect title cannot be abandoned under Pennsylvania law. Affirmed.

**Rules**
- **Abandonment of Property.** Abandoned property is that "to which an owner has voluntarily relinquished all right, title, claim and possession with the intention of terminating his ownership, but without vesting it in any other person and with the intention of not reclaiming further possession or resuming ownership, possession or enjoyment."
- **Abandonment of Perfect Title.** The doctrine of abandonment does not apply to perfect titles in real property.
- **Presumption of Possession.** Absent proof to the contrary, possession is presumed to be in the party who has record title.
- **Non-payment of Taxes/Non-use.** Abandonment of title is not presumed from a mere failure to possess the land or from neglect to pay the taxes thereon.
- **Rejected: Intent to Abandon.** Intent is an element in the doctrine of abandonment only if the claim survives a motion for summary judgment, and only in cases involving personal property.

**Reasoning**
- The MacKenzies have not relinquished their rights, title, claim, and possession of their lot; they remain owners in fee simple with a recorded deed and perfect title.
- Pennsylvania law does not allow for the abandonment of real property when owned in fee simple with perfect title.
- The MacKenzies' actions, such as non-use, refusal to pay taxes, and offers to sell, do not constitute abandonment of perfect title.
- The MacKenzies' intent to abandon is irrelevant because the law states that real property cannot be abandoned.
- The deed contains a covenant requiring the MacKenzies, as property owners, to pay annual dues and assessments to the Association.

**Dissent ([Judge])** / **Concurrence ([Judge])**
- None.

**NOTES:**
1. This case establishes a bright-line rule that **perfect title** to real property cannot be abandoned, contrasting with cases like *Ghen v. Rich* where abandonment of personal property (a whale) was possible. It highlights the strong protection afforded to record title in real estate, even against the owner's express intent to abandon. This case is similar to *Eyerman v. Mercantile Trust Co.* in that it limits an owner's ability to dispose of property in a way that conflicts with established legal principles, even if the owner desires it.
2. What if the MacKenzies had tried to convey the property to the county or state, and they had accepted it? Would that be considered abandonment or a transfer of title?

**Takeaway:** Under Pennsylvania law, an owner cannot abandon real property held in fee simple with perfect title, regardless of their intent or actions like non-use and non-payment of taxes.
