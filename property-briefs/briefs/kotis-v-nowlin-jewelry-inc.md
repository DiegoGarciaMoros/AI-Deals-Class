## Kotis v. Nowlin Jewelry Inc. (Texas Courts of Appeals, 1992)

**Facts**
- On June 11, 1990, Steve Sitton acquired a gold ladies Rolex watch from Nowlin Jewelry by forging a check belonging to his brother and misrepresenting his brother's authorization.
- The next day, Sitton telephoned Eddie Kotis, a used car dealership owner, and asked if he was interested in buying a Rolex watch.
- Kotis purchased the watch from Sitton for $3,550.00.
- Kotis called Nowlin Jewelry that same day and spoke with Cherie Nowlin, asking if a gold President model Rolex watch had been sold about a month prior.
- Kotis initially refused to identify himself to Ms. Nowlin.
- Ms. Nowlin told Kotis that Sitton had purchased the watch the day before.
- Ms. Nowlin called Kotis back and informed him that Sitton's check had not yet cleared.
- Kotis told Ms. Nowlin that he did not have the watch and did not want the watch, and refused to state Sitton's asking price.
- On June 15, 1990, Nowlin's bookkeeper learned that Sitton's check would be dishonored.
- John Nowlin, president of Nowlin's, called Kotis, but Kotis refused to speak with him and referred him to his attorney.
- On June 25, 1990, Kotis' attorney suggested Nowlin could buy the watch back from Kotis, which Nowlin refused.
- Sitton was indicted for forgery and theft.
- The district court ordered Nowlin's Jewelry to hold the watch until ownership was adjudicated.
- Nowlin filed suit seeking a declaratory judgment of ownership.
- Kotis filed a counterclaim for a declaration that he was a good faith purchaser.

**Procedural History**
- Nowlin Jewelry, Inc. filed suit seeking a declaratory judgment of ownership of the watch.
- Kotis filed a counterclaim.
- After a bench trial, the trial court rendered judgment declaring Nowlin the sole owner of the watch and awarded attorney's fees to Nowlin.
- Kotis appealed the trial court's judgment.

**Issue**
- Can a buyer who purchases goods from a swindler with voidable title acquire good title if the buyer did not act in good faith?

**Holding**
- Defendant (Nowlin Jewelry, Inc.) wins. A buyer who purchases goods from a swindler with voidable title cannot acquire good title if the buyer did not act in good faith. Affirmed.

**Rules**
- **UCC § 2.403(a) (Power to Transfer; Good Faith Purchase of Goods; "Entrusting").** A purchaser of goods acquires all title which his transferor had or had power to transfer, and a person with voidable title has power to transfer good title to a good faith purchaser for value.
- **UCC § 1.201(32) ("Purchase").** A "purchase" is a taking by sale, discount, negotiation, mortgage, pledge, lien, issue or reissue, gift or any other voluntary transaction creating an interest in property.
- **Void Title.** A thief who wrongfully takes goods against the will of the owner acquires only void title.
- **Voidable Title.** A swindler who fraudulently induces the victim to deliver goods voluntarily acquires voidable title.
- **UCC § 2.403(a)(4).** A purchaser can obtain voidable title even if delivery was procured through fraud punishable as larcenous under criminal law.
- **UCC § 1.201(19) ("Good Faith").** "Good faith" means honesty in fact in the conduct or transaction concerned.
- **Test for Good Faith.** The test for good faith is the actual belief of the party, not the reasonableness of that belief.
- **Evidence of Lack of Good Faith.** An unreasonably low price for goods is evidence the buyer knows the goods are stolen.

**Reasoning**
- Sitton obtained voidable title to the watch from Nowlin Jewelry.
    - Nowlin voluntarily delivered the watch to Sitton, even though Sitton used a forged check.
    - Sitton fraudulently induced Nowlin to deliver the watch, rather than taking it against Nowlin's will.
    - Under UCC § 2.403(a)(4), delivery procured through fraud punishable as larcenous still results in voidable title.
- Kotis did not act in good faith when purchasing the watch from Sitton.
    - The trial court, as the trier of fact, was the sole judge of witness credibility.
    - Kotis's testimony that he had already purchased the watch before learning Sitton's story was false could have been disbelieved by the judge.
    - Other facts supported the finding of lack of good faith:
        - Kotis initially refused to identify himself to Cherie Nowlin.
        - Kotis told Ms. Nowlin he did not have the watch and did not want it, despite having already purchased it.
        - Kotis refused to divulge Sitton's asking price.
        - Kotis later refused to speak with John Nowlin and referred him to his attorney.
    - Even if Kotis had already purchased the watch when he called Nowlin's, the extremely low price ($3,550 for a watch worth $7,000-$8,000) was evidence that Kotis knew the transaction was unlawful.
    - While the test for good faith is actual belief, it does not sanction willful disregard of suspicious facts.
- Because Kotis was not a good faith purchaser, he could not acquire good title from Sitton, who had only voidable title.
- Therefore, Nowlin Jewelry retained title and was entitled to possession of the watch.

**NOTES:**
1. This case illustrates the **good faith purchaser for value** doctrine under the UCC, specifically how it applies to goods obtained by fraud. It distinguishes between void and voidable title, a concept that is crucial for understanding the security of transactions involving personal property. It connects to the concept of **first possession** and how title can be transferred, even if imperfectly.
