OPINION

I. STANDARD OF REVIEW

We review de novo a district court's dismissal of a complaint under Rule
12(b)(6). To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain "enough
facts to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face." Bell Atlantic
Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 570, 127 S. Ct. 1955, 167 L. Ed. 2d 929 (2007).
A claim is facially plausible "when the plaintiff pleads factual content that
allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable
for the misconduct alleged." Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009).
Threadbare recitals of the elements of a cause of action do not suffice. Id.
Although we accept the complaint's factual allegations as true, we are "not
bound to accept as true a legal conclusion couched as a factual allegation."
Twombly, 550 U.S. at 555 (quoting Papasan v. Allain, 478 U.S. 265, 286 (1986)).

II. DISCUSSION

A. Section 1983

Plaintiff's first claim arises under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, which supplies a remedy
against any person who, under color of state law, deprives another of rights
secured by the Constitution. See Monroe v. Pape, 365 U.S. 167, 171-72 (1961),
overruled in part by Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658
(1978). A private party acts under color of state law only in limited
circumstances. See, e.g., Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922, 937 (1982)
(articulating two-part test for attributing private conduct to the State);
Flagg Bros., Inc. v. Brooks, 436 U.S. 149, 157 (1978). Because Acme is a
private corporation and the complaint alleges no facts suggesting joint
action with state officials, the claim fails. Cf. Ciambriello v. County of
Nassau, 292 F.3d 307, 324 (2d Cir. 2002) (conclusory allegations of
conspiracy insufficient). The district court correctly applied Lugar, 457
U.S. at 939, and we see no reason to disturb its analysis. Monell, supra, at
690, does not alter that conclusion.

B. Title VII

Plaintiff's second claim invokes Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42
U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a). The governing regulations define sexual harassment
broadly. 29 C.F.R. § 1604.11(a). To state a hostile work environment claim,
a plaintiff must plead conduct "sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the
conditions of [the victim's] employment." Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v.
Vinson, 477 U.S. 57, 67 (1986) (alteration in original) (internal quotation
marks omitted); accord Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17, 21
(1993). Whether an environment is hostile depends on the totality of the
circumstances. Harris, 510 U.S. at 23. We have applied that standard
repeatedly. See Littlejohn v. City of New York, 795 F.3d 297, 320-21 (2d Cir.
2015); Patane v. Clark, 508 F.3d 106, 113 (2d Cir. 2007). As we explained in
Littlejohn, a plaintiff need not plead a prima facie case. 795 F.3d at 311.
Id. at 312. Under Littlejohn, the complaint here suffices.

Commentators have long debated the reach of these rules. See generally
Arthur R. Miller, From Conley to Twombly to Iqbal: A Double Play on the
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, 60 Duke L.J. 1 (2010); Samuel Warren & Louis
Brandeis, The Right to Privacy, 4 Harv. L. Rev. 193 (1890).

C. Remaining Arguments

We have considered plaintiff's remaining arguments and find them without
merit. Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 679; Twombly, 550 U.S. at 556. See also Bell
Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544 (2007). But see Swierkiewicz v.
Sorema N.A., 534 U.S. 506, 512 (2002).

III. CONCLUSION

The judgment of the district court is AFFIRMED as to the § 1983 claim and
VACATED and REMANDED as to the Title VII claim. Smith, supra, is not to the
contrary.
